Light of the Lighthouse

Anna Anopova

English translation in progress. This is a preview of the translated preface, not the complete book. The full original is available in Russian.

Preface

My grandmother Zina used to say:

“Life is no field to cross.”

I think that is what each of our journeys looks like. Sometimes the path stretches out before us, wide and open. Sometimes it becomes so difficult that we cannot see where to take the next step.

Dear Grandma, I love you so much. And I know you are always near. Just as those who are no longer physically here are near, too, their love living within me every day.

I have had to live through things most people would rather not speak about. And I know the value of silence. But I also know how important it is, in the hardest moments, to have someone beside you who is willing to share that silence. Someone who does not run away, look aside, or rush to find the right words. Someone who can listen to your thoughts, share moments of sadness, and simply stay, without trying to “fix” the pain or make it smaller.

I am writing this book for those who need someone like that beside them right now.

This is a book about love.

About the love we search for, sometimes without knowing how to notice that it is already there. About the love we try to hold on to because we are afraid of losing it. About love for our children, which changes how we understand ourselves and the world. About a love that does not disappear, even when everything that once made up our lives changes.

This book holds my motherhood, my relationships, encounters and partings. It holds joy, exhaustion, mistakes, and discoveries that took me years to reach.

And it holds a loss that is still difficult for me to speak about.

I have worked as a psychologist for many years. But when it came to my own life, I discovered that knowledge and experience do not provide answers to the questions that matter most. I did not know the right way to live through loss, how much time it would take to feel joy again, or whether there is any right way at all to live through what happens to us.

I do not want to turn what I have lived through into a set of neat conclusions.

I want to tell my story. Honestly. Without hiding its contradictions, without trying to make myself seem better than I was, and without insisting that everything that happened must have a meaning.

Perhaps someone will recognize themselves in this story. Perhaps it will help someone feel that, even in our most difficult experiences, we are not always as alone as we might think.

I want there to be room here for everything: for love and anger, tenderness and disappointment, gratitude and regret. For questions that do not yet have answers.

I am also writing for those who are no longer physically beside me, but whose love has stayed with me.

For my grandmother.

And above all, for my Marusya.

This book is my conversation with her, across space and time.

I do not know what lies beyond the limits of our earthly experience. But I know that the love I feel for my daughter did not end when I could no longer hold her, hear her voice, or watch her grow.

It continues to live within me, changing me, my relationships with others, and the way I see the world.

And perhaps that is why, one day, I was able to begin writing.
